- **19:30** The fundamentals
  id:: 67e3e602-8c47-4b51-b768-16c7d47a8ab5
  collapsed:: true
	- The fundamentals of AI that are worth understanding:
	  id:: 67e3e602-da19-410a-bfb7-7a904d4c2b5a
	- 1. AI systems are pattern-matching tools trained on human-created data. They're powerful at recognizing and extending patterns, but they don't have goals, desires, or consciousness.
	- 2. Current AI progress is happening through scaling existing approaches (larger models, more data, more computing) rather than fundamental breakthroughs in how AI works.
	- 3. These systems excel at tasks involving language, images, and information processing, but they struggle with physical tasks, causal reasoning, and true understanding.
	  4. AI is changing knowledge work first - writing, coding, design, data analysis - before physical work, though robotics is advancing too.
	  5. The most consequential near-term impacts are on labor markets and information ecosystems - changing what jobs look like and how we determine what's true.
	  6. These technologies amplify existing power structures unless deliberately designed otherwise - the organizations with data, computing, and technical talent gain advantages.
	- The core question isn't "what can this technology do?" but rather "how do we want to integrate this technology into human systems in ways that benefit society?"
	- Understanding these basics gives you a foundation for interpreting new developments without needing to track every announcement.
- **19:45** How is it different from machine learning?
  id:: 67e3e602-1560-4461-a033-bbc52120b2f2
  collapsed:: true
	- AI and machine learning relate to each other as follows:
	  Machine learning is actually a subset of AI - think of it as a specific approach to creating artificial intelligence. It's the part that's driving most of today's headline-making advances.
	- The key difference is in how they work:
	- Traditional AI (before machine learning became dominant) relied on explicit rules programmed by humans. Engineers would code specific instructions for every situation the system might encounter.
	- Machine learning flips this approach. Instead of programming rules directly, we create systems that learn patterns from data. Show a machine learning system thousands of labeled examples, and it discovers the patterns on its own.
	  Modern AI systems like ChatGPT, Claude, and image generators are based on a specific type of machine learning called deep learning - which uses neural networks loosely inspired by the human brain's structure.
	- The breakthrough that's caused the recent explosion is that these machine learning systems, when scaled up enormously (with more data, computing power, and model size), suddenly develop capabilities that weren't explicitly programmed.
	- So while "AI" is technically the broader category, today's AI revolution is specifically driven by advances in large-scale machine learning.

	- **LLMs (Large Language Models)** and **Reasoning Models** differ primarily in their approach to problem-solving:
	- **LLMs**: These models are trained on vast datasets to recognize patterns and generate text based on statistical probabilities. They excel at tasks like text generation and simple question-answering but may struggle with complex logical reasoning[2][6].
	- **Reasoning Models**: Designed to simulate logical processes, these models use explicit knowledge representation and inference mechanisms. They are capable of multi-step reasoning, often displaying intermediate steps in their thought process, making them more suitable for tasks requiring structured decision-making[1][3][7].
	  
	  While LLMs can mimic certain types of reasoning, they don't truly "reason" in the way humans do; instead, they rely on pattern recognition[5][6].

- **20:38** What about when AGI emerges? {{cloze (Follow-up to: ((67e3f044-66b9-47ba-90d7-959ec9da70a6)))}}
  id:: 67e3f543-1775-4177-af6c-17c7b3c8f7fa
  collapsed:: true
	- When considering AGI (Artificial General Intelligence), it's important to distinguish between different scenarios:
	  
	  If AGI emerges gradually over many years, your business analyst role will likely evolve rather than disappear. You'd shift toward higher-level decision-making, strategic planning, and uniquely human skills like navigating organizational politics, building trust, and facilitating complex negotiations between stakeholders.
	  
	  If AGI emerges more rapidly and can truly perform all cognitive tasks at human level or beyond, career planning becomes more speculative. In this scenario, the most valuable roles would center around:
	  
	  1. Defining what we want these systems to do (setting goals and priorities)
	  2. Verifying the systems are behaving as intended (oversight and governance)
	  3. Areas where we specifically prefer human involvement regardless of capability
	- The skills that would serve you well in either scenario include:
	  id:: 67e3f5da-7542-4a70-b683-55ec368da38c
		- ==Adaptability and continuous learning==
		- ==Ethical reasoning and value judgment==
		- ==Complex communication and relationship-building==
		- ==Understanding organisational dynamics==
	- The timeline matters tremendously here. Most serious AI researchers suggest fully capable AGI is still years or decades away, giving you time to adapt as the transition unfolds.
	- ==For practical purposes, focusing on becoming excellent at working with today's AI tools to solve business problems is your best near-term strategy, while developing the uniquely human skills that would remain valuable even in a world with advanced AGI.==
