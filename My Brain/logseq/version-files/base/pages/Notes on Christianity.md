- Incarnation of Christ
	- Chalcedonian Definition: Christ is 'acknowledged in two natures', which 'come together into one person and one hypostasis'
	- Hypostasis (theology): one of the three elements of the trinity
	- Council of Chalcedon in A.D. 451
	- Monophysitism vs. Dyophysitism
	- Non-Chalcedonian: Mono or Mia
	- Chalcedonian: Dyo
- Miaphysitism vs. Monophysitism
	- Mia - 'one' or 'single"
	- Physis - 'nature'
	- Mono - 'only, single'
	- Miaphysitism
		- Used by Oriental Orthodox Churches
		- Divinity and Humanity united in 'one' or 'single' nature and they are united without separation, without confusion, and without alternation
		- Non-Chalcedonian: 'out of two natures'
	- Monophysitism
		- Generic term: Miaphysitism, a form of Monophysitism
			- Monophysites: a term for non-Chalcedonian Miaphysites
			- monophysitism: generic, heretical
	- 'Only' a 'single' nature which was either divine (or a synthesis of divine and human)
	- Chalcedonian: 'in two natures'
- Dyophysitism
	- Two natures, human and divine, exist in one person of Jesus
- Nature in contemporary theology: emphasis on the concrete, historical, and social aspects
	- Concrete: capable of being perceived by the senses, not abstract or imaginary
	- Generally, refers to the created universe (rational or irrational or both) with determined laws of interdependence and God as its source and end.
	- Specifically, not only refers to the philosophical definition of a being's species and proper activity but also a deeper understanding from revelation of the concrete nature of man, angel and God.
