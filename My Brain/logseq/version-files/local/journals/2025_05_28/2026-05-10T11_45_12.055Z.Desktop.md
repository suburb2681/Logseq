	- We’ve all experienced circumstances that caused us to blame others - mistakes, disappointments, or various forms of hurt will cause us to point the finger. As Pema Chödrön describes it, ‘Blame is a very common, ancient, well perfected device for trying to feel better.’ In an effort to avert responsibility and protect ourselves from pain, we blame other people, society, even fate. Take an example of someone who struggled with debt and financial turmoil for years. One day they receive a bill from a creditor and it triggers a surge of anger. Stewing in frustration, they project responsibility onto something else. ==Perhaps they point the finger at their upbringing, their boss, a person who wronged them, a society that overlooked them.== Looking outward to blame external factors, there is some momentary relief. But the truth is blaming doesn’t work and it leads to inevitable suffering. ==By projecting responsibility outward, we leave ourselves feeling powerless. If we never feel responsible, we don’t take action to change the things that are within our control.== Our pain increases. The cycle continues. We find something else to blame and the unwanted circumstances persist. When we realise that pointing the finger doesn’t help, it’s natural to turn the blame towards ourselves. However this just leaves us feeling incompetent and damages our self-worth. ==The idea is to release all blame to take responsibility and to accept the situation as it is. Personal responsibility is rooted in honesty, compassion and acceptance, and it gives us the strength to move forward.== It’s not easy but ==by finding our own responsibility, we break the cycle of blame and recognise that in this moment - only WE can change the situation because it’s OUR situation.== As a venerable Ajahn Chah said, ’Blaming somebody else for your suffering is like having an itch on your head and scratching your bottom. Now you have 2 itches for the price of one.’ ==So the next time you feel that impulse to blame, ask yourself if you have any responsibility in what’s happening. Remember here you are. What are you gonna do? What is there to learn? There is nowhere to go but forward.==
