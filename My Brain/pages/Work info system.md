- Oh, I feel that scattered system struggle! When you've got email flags, bullet journal, AND OneNote all going at once, it's like having three different assistants who don't talk to each other.
  
  This might be a perfect scenario for applying the "one inbox" principle - not necessarily meaning one tool, but one consistent decision-making process across your tools:
  
  1. **Create a unified workflow** - Maybe emails stay in email (with your flags), but add a daily ritual where you review them and add critical ones to your OneNote hub with just enough context to know what's where
  
  2. **Designate "home bases" for different types of information**:
	- Email: External communications only
	- Bullet journal: Daily tasks and personal reminders
	- OneNote: Project thinking, meeting notes, and your "second brain" content
	  
	  3. **Cross-reference between systems** with a simple code - like "(BJ-3/24)" in OneNote to indicate "check bullet journal entry from March 24"
	  
	  4. **Weekly review all three systems** at once - maybe Friday afternoon, to ensure nothing's falling between the cracks
	  
	  5. **Create a simple diagram** of what goes where and post it near your workspace - this reduces the mental load of deciding where each piece of information belongs
	  
	  The goal isn't necessarily to force everything into one system (which might be impossible with work restrictions anyway), but to create clear lanes for different types of information so your brain isn't constantly switching contexts to remember where something might be.
	  
	  Sometimes embracing a hybrid approach and just being intentional about it can work better than fighting against the realities of your work environment!
